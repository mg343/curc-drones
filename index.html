01 / THE IDEAWHY DRONES

You can buy
a drone.
You can’t buy
the understanding.

A drone is the most compact way to learn the whole hardware stack at once.

One aircraft, and it only flies if every discipline in it works. We build the parts other teams buy because building them is the only way to understand them.

The country needs engineers who understand machines all the way down. That’s who we’re trying to become.

LAND — WP 03

DESIGNED IN-HOUSEOFF THE SHELF

  1. 01 / 07IN-HOUSE · MECHANICAL

    Frame

    Our own 5-inch frame, designed from scratch: the bottom plate and arms are modelled in CAD around how load runs from the motors into the stack. Prototypes are printed and tested for strength against weight, and the final frame is cast in carbon fibre.

    CLASS
    5-INCH
    MOTOR MOUNT
    16 × 16 MM, M3
    PROCESS
    CAD → PRINT → CARBON CAST

  2. 02 / 07IN-HOUSE · MECHANICAL

    Propellers

    Designed from the aerodynamics up and modelled in CAD. Before anything is printed, each design goes through simulation: FEA for how the blades bend and stress under load, and airflow modelling for how much thrust they actually make. The designs that hold up are printed on the Makerspace SLS printers and tested against the numbers.

    DIAMETER
    5 IN / 127 MM
    PROCESS
    SLS PRINT
    SIMULATION
    FEA + AIRFLOW

  3. 03 / 07OFF THE SHELF

    Motors

    The one spinning part we buy. Winding motors wouldn’t teach much. Designing the electronics that drive them does.

    MODEL
    VELOX V2207 V3
    KV
    1750
    PEAK
    ~35 A ON 6S

  4. 04 / 07IN-HOUSE · ELECTRICAL

    Motor controllers

    Our own ESCs (electronic speed controllers). A microcontroller and a gate driver switch three half-bridges in sequence to spin each brushless motor, timing the commutation off the motor’s back-EMF. Schematic and layout are done in Altium, and every circuit is proven on the bench before it becomes a custom PCB.

    MCU
    EFM8BB21
    GATE DRIVER
    FD6288Q
    NOW
    BENCH BRING-UP

  5. 05 / 07IN-HOUSE · ELECTRICAL

    Flight controller

    An ESP32-S3 reads the gyro and accelerometer on a six-axis IMU, fuses them into an estimate of which way the aircraft is pointing, and turns the error into four motor commands, hundreds of times a second. The PID is simulated and tuned in MATLAB first, so the first flight isn’t the first test.

    MCU
    ESP32-S3
    IMU
    ICM-42688-P
    NOW
    FIRST FIRMWARE

  6. 06 / 07IN-HOUSE · ELECTRICAL

    Radio

    Two 2.4 GHz transceivers: one on the aircraft, one in a handheld controller we’re building from scratch with gimbal sticks, a Li-ion pack and a printed shell. The packet format, channel addressing and a failsafe for signal loss are designed together with the flight controller.

    LINK
    NRF24L01+ PA+LNA
    HANDHELD
    ATMEGA32U4
    NOW
    PROTOTYPING

  7. 07 / 07OFF THE SHELF

    Battery

    A 6S pack, chosen from day one so that adding a payload later never means redesigning the power system.

    PACK
    6S 1100 MAH LIPO
    MAX
    25.2 V
    PLUG
    XT60

  8. NEXT

    Then it has to fly.

    A first controlled flight is the target for this semester. Autonomy comes next.
