# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
Anyone who lands on the page: mostly Columbia students, plus potential mentors and partners. Their job is to understand, quickly and without fluff, what this club is and does. A visitor who wants in emails the team.

## Product Purpose
The site of CURC Drones, a student team in the Columbia University Robotics Club founded in September 2026. It explains what the team is and does. Success means a visitor leaves knowing four things: this team builds drones from scratch, starting from the math and physics; it designs every major subsystem itself; it does this to understand how flight actually works; and joining means emailing.

## Positioning
Not a kit-flying or drone-racing club. Students design and build every major subsystem: the frame, propellers, motor controllers, flight controller and radio link. The point is to understand the whole stack end to end, starting from first principles: state estimation, control loops, power electronics, RF and structures.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- A single static page with no build step, no framework, no tracking, and no downloaded fonts (Arial, Georgia and Courier New only). The pushed header and hollow #drones masthead are fixed.
- Native scrolling. Never take over scrolling or scroll speed.
- Contact: mailto:mihir.garimella@columbia.edu. Updates: https://x.com/ColumbiaDrones.

## Brand Commitments
- Name: CURC Drones (Columbia University Robotics Club). The wordmark is #drones.
- Assets: assets/curc-logo.png (the club's crown-robot logo).
- Voice: plain and direct, with no fluff. Conviction about re-industrialization and building hardware from first principles has to feel earned, not hyped.

## Evidence on Hand
The club's own records (Oct 2026): the real parts list and first order ($279.86), workstream leads, meeting cadence (Fridays 3–5 PM, Makerspace), the timeline from the Sept 18 first meeting, and competition goals (IMAV, SUAS, cUASC, ICRA/IROS aerial tracks). Use these; invent nothing beyond them.

## Product Principles
1. Say what the club is before saying why it matters.
2. Show the engineering rather than describing it: the subsystems and the physics behind them are the substance.
3. Every element has to help a visitor understand the club. Cut decoration that doesn't.
4. Be honest about being new. Claim no history.
